Golden, juicy rotisserie chicken on the spit with a simple spice rub, dry-brined for shatter-crisp skin.

- **Yield:** 1 (4–5 lb) chicken
- **Dry brine time:** 4 hours
- **Cook time:** 1½–2 hours
- **Rest time:** 10 minutes

## Ingredients
- 1 whole chicken (4–5 lb)
- 1 tablespoon kosher salt (roughly ¾ tsp per pound of bird)
- 1 teaspoon freshly cracked black pepper
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon smoked or sweet paprika (gives the chicken that classic golden-red rotisserie color)
- ½ teaspoon dried thyme or rosemary
- Giblets (neck, gizzard, heart) — optional, if making gravy

## Instructions
1. **Make the rub:** Combine the kosher salt, black pepper, garlic powder, paprika, and dried thyme or rosemary in a small bowl.
2. **Season:** Remove the giblets from the cavity and set them aside in the refrigerator if you plan on making gravy. Pat the chicken dry with paper towels, then season it all over, including the cavity, with the rub. The salt amount is sized for a 4–5 lb bird, about ¾ tsp per pound.
3. **Dry brine:** Set the chicken on a wire rack over a sheet pan and refrigerate, uncovered, for 4 hours. The salt penetrates the meat while the cold fridge air dries out the skin for the crispiest results.
4. **Truss:** The bird must be trussed. Tuck the wing tips behind the shoulders and tie the legs snugly against the body so nothing flaps or snags as the spit turns.
5. **Preheat:** Set up your grill for rotisserie cooking and heat it to 375°F.
6. **Drip boat:** Form a boat out of aluminum foil and set it on top of the grilling racks, positioned below where the spit will run, to catch the drippings.
7. **Mount and cook:** Slide the bird onto the spit, centering and balancing it, and secure it with the forks. Start the spit, close the lid, and roast. Starting from a refrigerated bird, expect 1½–2 hours on the spit.
8. **Add the giblets (if making gravy):** With 40 minutes left on the clock, add the reserved giblets — neck, gizzard, and heart — to the drippings in the foil boat so they cook into the drippings. Discard the liver; it does not go into the drippings.
9. **Check doneness:** The chicken is done when the thigh meat in the gap between the drumstick and the breast reads 160–165°F. Don't judge it by time alone.
10. **Rest:** Remove the chicken from the spit and let it rest for 10 minutes before carving. The drippings in the foil boat can be used to make gravy.

## Notes
- **Doneness:** Always use a thermometer, checking the gap between the drumstick and the breast. Pull at 160–165°F; the bird will coast a few degrees while resting.
- **Crisp skin:** The dry brine is the key to crisp skin. Don't skip the uncovered rest in the fridge.
- **Liver:** Always discard the liver — it never goes into the drip pan.
- **Gravy:** The drippings, with the giblets cooked in, are a great base for gravy. A recipe for that is coming later.
